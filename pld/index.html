EU Product Liability Directive Kit

Directive (EU) 2024/2853 · Toledo Technologies LLC

On 8 December 2026, software becomes a product.

Directive (EU) 2024/2853 replaces the 1985 product liability regime. Software, SaaS and AI systems are explicitly “products”. Liability is no-fault: a claimant proves defect, damage and causation — not that you were negligent. The old “the defect appeared later” defence is narrowed where you keep control through updates, and the inability to supply the security updates needed to keep a product safe can be relevant to whether it is defective.

Article 2(1) as published said 9 December 2026. Corrigendum 2026/90364 corrected it to 8 December. Articles 20–22 still say 9 December — those govern transposition and the repeal of 85/374/EEC, not scope. Most commentary still has this wrong.

What this kit refuses to do

It is not legal advice, not a certification, and not a compliance determination. Whether a product is defective is decided by a court on the facts.

Where a question genuinely needs a lawyer, the kit says so instead of guessing. There are 25 explicit [VERIFY: …] markers naming exactly what to check and where.

It also tells you things that do not help me sell it. Recital 51 says the directive imposes no obligation to provide updates — there is no patching mandate here, and the kit says so in bold. Recital 24 excludes pure economic loss, so if you are B2B SaaS and your failure mode is downtime rather than personal injury or property damage, your exposure under this directive may be low. That is in the kit too.
